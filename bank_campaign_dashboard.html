The data and the traps in it

UCI Bank Marketing: 41,176 phone contacts after removing 12 duplicates. One row is one call to one client. The task is to predict, before the call, whether the client subscribes.

  • The code 999 in pdays means "never contacted" (96.3% of rows). It became a yes/no flag.
  • "Unknown" answers in job, education, default, housing and loan were kept as their own category.
  • The number of contacts in this campaign was capped at 10 because a few clients had up to 56.

Five models, compared fairly

Every model was tested with stratified 5-fold cross-validation on the training data, with all preprocessing inside the pipeline. The test set was used once at the end.

Random Forest and Gradient Boosting are close. The tuned Gradient Boosting model was chosen because it had the best cross-validated PR-AUC.

SMOTE did not help. Gradient Boosting scored 0.463 PR-AUC without it and 0.453 with it, so imbalance is handled with a cost-based cut-off instead.

Which customers rise to the top

Fairness check on age

Selection follows real yes-rates, but strongly at the extremes. All 110 customers over 65 were selected, which needs human review for a financial product.

Recommendation and limits

What the bank should do

  • Rank customers with the tuned Gradient Boosting model and call roughly the top 20%. On test data that reaches about two thirds of subscribers.
  • Prioritise earlier successes, cellular contacts, students and retired clients, and March, September and October. Cut mass calling in May.
  • Pilot first: top 20% against a random group, then compare real conversion.
  • Replace the assumed costs with real ones and recompute the cut-off.
  • Add human review before contacting older customers, and retrain regularly.

What to keep in mind

Economic indicators dominate. The data run from 2008 to 2010 and the model leans on indicators such as nr.employed. They partly stand in for the time period, so a random split probably makes scores look better than they would on a future campaign.
  • One hold-out set with 928 subscribers, so scores carry some uncertainty.
  • Profit depends on assumed values (call cost 1, subscription worth 10).
  • The model finds patterns, not causes.
  • Probabilities were used to rank and were not checked for calibration.
